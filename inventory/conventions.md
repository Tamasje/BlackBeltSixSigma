### MSA / Gage R&R

- **Gauge R&R (MSA) aanbevolen drempels**: Resolutie <= 10% van tolerantie om te meten. Gauge R&R <= 20% van tolerantie om te meten. Repeatability (Equipment variation): variatie met 1 meetdevice, herhaald door 1 operator, op hetzelfde deel. Reproducibility (Appraised variation): variatie tussen verschillende operatoren met hetzelfde toestel op hetzelfde deel. Stability or Drift: totale variatie t.o.v. een master/referentiewaarde over uitgestrekte tijd. (source/course/Les 4/2026 Lean - Six Sigma v13 - Capabiliteit - SPC.pdf p. 61, S06)
- **attribute MSA acceptance / repeatability-reproducibility procedure**: Set aside 15 to 30 samples (about half pass, half fail); create a 'master' standard via a panel of experts; pick two or three inspectors; have each inspector repeat measurements after randomizing order; calculate percentage agreement of first vs second measurements per inspector (repeatability), and agreement across inspectors and with the master standard (reproducibility). Example given: a calculated 63 percent agreement between all inspectors for all samples with the 'master' standard means there is a 63 percent likelihood the measurement system will correctly measure items and a 37 percent chance of error. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 177, S08)
- **measurement-system variance ratio acceptance thresholds**: sigma^2_measure/sigma^2_observed <= 0.1: good measurement system, use as is. 0.1 to 0.3: marginal, use with caution only if no better alternative exists. >= 0.3: unacceptable measurement system, must be corrected before use. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 178, S08)
- **Gage R&R / MSA sampling guidance**: Valid estimates of the variance of the measurement system usually involve two to three inspectors and five to ten process outputs or characteristics to measure. Each inspector also measures each process output or characteristic two to three times. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 179, S08)
- **5.15 sigma vs 6 sigma study variation**: Footnote on slide 24: 'Historically, by convention, a 99 % spread has been used to represent the “full spread of the measurement error”. Following this convention, a multiplying factor of 5.15 has to be used. More recently a multiplying factor of 6 is used, representing 99.73 % spread.' The formulas actually printed on the slides (%GRR = 6*sigma_m/6*sigma_o and %GRR = 6*sigma_m/TOL) use the factor 6, not 5.15. (source/course/Les 5/20260619_ottoy_Black Belt in Six Sigma - Measurement System Analysis.pdf p. 24, S10)
- **%GRR relative to total variation vs relative to tolerance**: Both bases are taught: '%GRR based on process variation' = 6*sigma_m/6*sigma_o (x100%) and '%GRR based on tolerance range' = 6*sigma_m/TOL (x100%) (slide 24). ### Acceptance sampling

- **Default alpha/beta for constructing an (n,c) plan from AQL and LQL (Peach method)**: AQL = p such that OC(p) = 1 - alpha (= 95 %). LQL = p such that OC(p) = beta (= 10 %). 'normally alpha = 5%... In the same way for the LQL (LTPD), normally taken beta = 10%.' (source/course/Les 2/20260529_ottoy_Acceptance Sampling - Further Reading.pdf p. 4, S04)
- **Distribution used for the OC-curve of attribute sampling plans**: OC(p) = P(X<=c|p) = H(c;N,Mp,n) with Mp=[Np]; 'H = the hypergeometric c.d.f.' For SPRT: '(For binomial distribution instead of hypergeometric, OK if lot size is large compared to sample size.)' (source/course/Les 2/20260529_ottoy_Acceptance Sampling - Further Reading.pdf p. 4, S04)
- **Rectifying inspection definitions (AOQ, AOQL, ATI)**: AOQ(p) = pOC(p)(1-(n/N)Rs*) ~= pOC(p) for n<<N; 'AOQ = Average Outgoing Quality... The maximum value of the AOQ-curve is called the Average Outgoing Quality Limit (AOQL).' 'ATI stands for the Average Total Inspection effort to be made.' (source/course/Les 2/20260529_ottoy_Acceptance Sampling - Further Reading.pdf p. 10, S04)
- **AQL / LQL (LTPD) definitions**: AQL : acceptable quality level - should normally pass the inspection. LQL : limiting quality level (LTPD : lot tolerance percent defectives) - should normally be rejected. (Further Reading, p.2: 'Acceptable quality is described by the number AQL (acceptable quality level), while unacceptable quality is described by the number LQL (limiting quality level, synonym : LTPD = lot tolerance percent defectives). AQL and LQL should be agreed upon by both the parties involved.') (source/course/Les 2/20260529_ottoy_Acceptance Sampling.pdf p. 23, S04)
- **Standards referenced for acceptance sampling**: 'Acceptance sampling plans can be found in the ISO-standards 2859 (attributes) and 3951 (variables). These standards have evolved out of the MIL-STD-105 which in turn resulted out of the pioneering work by Harold Dodge and Harry Romig at Bell Labs in the 1920's.' Further Reading p.12 adds: ISO2859 indexes plans by lot size, inspection level (I/II/III), AQL -> Sample Size Code Letter (SSCL) -> sampling plan; severity states normal/tightened/reduced with formal transition rules; sampling procedure can be single/double/multiple/sequential. (source/course/Les 2/20260529_ottoy_Acceptance Sampling.pdf p. 23, S04)
- **Excel functions used in the acceptance-sampling workbook**: Cells use (with the openpyxl-visible _xlfn. prefix for post-2007 functions): _xlfn.NORM.DIST, _xlfn.NORM.INV, _xlfn.EXPON.DIST, _xlfn.HYPGEOM.DIST, _xlfn.RANK.EQ, _xlfn.STDEV.S, _xlfn.VAR.P, plus legacy COMBIN, RAND, SUMIF, SUMPRODUCT, VLOOKUP/HLOOKUP. ### Sigma level, 1.5σ shift, DPMO

- **1.5σ-shift / sigma-niveau tabel**: De slide toont een Sigma Level / Defects-per-Million / Yield tabel (bv. 6σ -> 3.4 DPM, 99.99966%) zonder expliciet te vermelden of dit lange-termijn cijfers zijn met een 1.5σ-verschuiving verondersteld. Zie ambiguities. (source/course/Les 1/20260521_van volsem.pdf p. 7, S01)
- **Deming's inspection-cost model assumption**: 'If the process is stable at a given percentage of defectives, then either no inspection or 100% inspection is cost optimal. Deming's assumption is that the production process is stable. Sampling plans do not make this assumption and are designed for catching shifts in process levels.' (source/course/Les 2/20260529_ottoy_Acceptance Sampling - Further Reading.pdf p. 15, S04)
- **1.5 sigma shift**: Motorola (waar Six Sigma ontstond) observeerde empirisch dat een procesgemiddelde over lange termijn ongeveer +/- 1,5 standaardafwijkingen kan verschuiven. Het is geen universele wet, maar (1) een empirische observatie van vroege industriële processen en (2) een conservatieve aanname om rekening te houden met reële-wereld variatie ('Even if your process looks perfect today, expect it to degrade over time.'). Technische definitie op de slide: bij Cp=Cpk=2 gecentreerd, en na 1,5 sigma shift Cp=2/Cpk=1,5, resulteert in 3,4 DPMO aan elke zijde (6 sigma to LSL / 6 sigma to USL). (source/course/Les 4/2026 Lean - Six Sigma v13 - Capabiliteit - SPC.pptx p. 37, S06)
- **Definition of Six Sigma performance / DPMO**: Six Sigma performance is the statistical term for a process that produces fewer than 3.4 defects (or errors) per million opportunities for defects. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 27, S07)
- **Rolled throughput yield (deferred formal treatment)**: In Six Sigma, we call this concept of compounding defect risk rolled throughput yield, and we explain it mathematically in Chapter 6. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 39, S07)
- **Sigma level to percent-defective/DPMO scale**: The sigma scale is a universal measure of how well a critical characteristic performs compared to its requirements. The higher the sigma score, the more capable the characteristic. ... Table 1-2 shows the sigma scale. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 41, S07)
- **1.5-sigma shift**: Early practitioners of Six Sigma proposed that mathematically shifting a characteristic's or process's short-term distribution closer to its specification limit by a distance of 1.5 times its short-term standard deviation (sigma_ST) would approximate the amount of defects occurring in the long term. ... Zlt = Zst - 1.5. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 159, S08)
- **Z-bench / sigma level <-> DPMO table**: See Table 6-3 (transcribed in constant_tables) and its printed note: paired table values are long-term for DPMO and short-term for Z; add 1.5 to corresponding Z values to obtain short-term equivalents. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 160, S08)
- **Defect / opportunity / DPMO definitions**: Glossary: 'DPMO (defects per million opportunities): The total number of defects observed divided by the total number of opportunities, expressed in events per million. Sometimes called Defects per Million (DPM)'; 'DPU (defects per unit): The total number of defects detected in some number of units divided by the total number of those units.'; 'opportunities: The number of characteristics, parameters, or features of a product or service that can be classified as acceptable or unacceptable.'; 'defect: An output of a process that fails to meet a defined specification or requirement...'; 'defective: A unit of product or service that contains at least one defect.' Also: Defects Per Million Opportunities defined as the total number of defects per unit divided by the total number of opportunities for defects per unit, multiplied by 1,000,000 (project-selection metric). (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 339, S09)
- **Sigma level <-> DPMO / cost of quality table**: The Cost of Quality: Sigma Level 2 = 308,537 dpm (noncompetitive companies), cost not applicable; 3 = 66,807 dpm, 25-40% of sales; 4 = 6,210 dpm (industry average), 15-25% of sales; 5 = 233 dpm, 5-15% of sales; 6 = 3.4 dpm (world class), <1% of sales. Each sigma shift provides a 10 percent net income improvement, a 20 percent margin improvement, and a 10 to 30 percent capital reduction. (source/course/Les 4/Six-Sigma Mikel Harry -  Richard Schroeder book summary.pdf p. 2, S09)
- **1.5 sigma shift**: All processes vary around their target mean. Over time, the variance increases due to 'process-centering error'. Research has shown that 4-sigma processes drift about 1.5-sigma over time. So when a company claims 6-sigma processes, they really mean: (a) the short-term sigma capability of their processes is 6-sigma (b) the long-term performance is 4.5-sigma due to process-centering errors. (source/course/Les 4/Six-Sigma Mikel Harry -  Richard Schroeder.pdf p. 7, S09)

This will be further explained in the section on SPC.' (source/course/Les 2/20260529_ottoy_Acceptance Sampling.pdf p. 10, S04)
- **Cp/Cpk vs Pp/Ppk**: Korte termijn: Cp / Cpk (common cause - process engineer). Lange termijn (waarin de klant geïnteresseerd is): Pp / Ppk (P = Performance). Cp/Cpk met korte termijn sigma gerekend - white noise (within variation); Pp/Ppk met lange termijn sigma gerekend - black noise, over time - belangrijk voor klant, de echte parameter. In Minitab-rapporten: 'Actual (overall)' = Pp/Ppk, 'Potential (within)' = Cp/Cpk. (source/course/Les 4/2026 Lean - Six Sigma v13 - Capabiliteit - SPC.pdf p. 33, S06)
- **6 sigma kwaliteitsniveau / interpretatie Cp**: Cp<1: proces niet capabel. Cp=1: net capabel (specificatie = UL-LL = 6sigma -> 99,73%, ~2700 op 10^6 buiten tolerantie). Cp>=1,33: acceptabel (specificatie=8sigma -> 99,9936%, ~60 op 10^6 buiten tolerantie, 64ppm). Cp>=1,67: goed (specificatie=10sigma -> 99,999943%, ~0,57 op 10^6 buiten tolerantie). Cp=2: 6 Sigma kwaliteitsniveau - 2 defect per miljard opportuniteiten (korte termijn). (source/course/Les 4/2026 Lean - Six Sigma v13 - Capabiliteit - SPC.pdf p. 40, S06)
- **Minimum aantal subgroepen voor controlekaarten / capability-analyse**: Slide (SPC process set up): 'Effective control charts need at least 20 subgroups of data.' Minitab-gegenereerde Report Cards (in de slides, o.a. p.49 en p.80): 'For a capability analysis, it is generally recommended that you collect at least 25 subgroups over a long enough period of time to capture the different sources of process variation.' Beide drempels (20 vs 25) komen voor in het cursusmateriaal zonder expliciete verzoening. (source/course/Les 4/2026 Lean - Six Sigma v13 - Capabiliteit - SPC.pdf p. 58, S06)
- **Eenzijdig vs tweezijdig denken**: Denken in eenzijdige sigma, niet in 2 zijdig, eenzijdig maakt de zaken eenvoudiger. (Slordige figuur - eenzijdig en 2-zijdig moeten apart worden uitgelegd; Symmetrische verdeling.) In de oefeningen wordt expliciet aangegeven wanneer 1-zijdig of 2-zijdig gerekend moet worden (bv. oefening capability p.46: 'maar je moet hier 2 zijdig rekenen'). (source/course/Les 4/2026 Lean - Six Sigma v13 - Capabiliteit - SPC.pptx p. 18, S06)
- **Cpk terminologie**: C = Capability, p = process, pk = process + Japans woord voor off-center. Cpk: Japans woord voor off-center. Ppk: P from performance. (source/course/Les 4/2026 Lean - Six Sigma v13 - Capabiliteit - SPC.pptx p. 34, S06)
- **Notatie: punt (.) als vermenigvuldigingsteken**: In de (Vlaamse) cursusnotatie wordt de komma (,) gebruikt als decimaalteken en het punt (.) soms als vermenigvuldigingsteken tussen twee getallen (bv. 'Cp = (72,8-71,4)/6.0,2 = 1,4/1,2' betekent 6 x 0,2; 'Cpk = min[(104-90)/(3.4);...]' betekent 3 x sigma(=4), niet het decimale getal 3,4). (source/course/Les 4/2026 Lean - Six Sigma v13 - Capabiliteit - SPC.pptx p. 46, S06)
- **Sigma-schatting voor capability (Cp/Cpk)**: sigma = Rbar / d2 (schatting op basis van gemiddelde range), met d2 uit tabel afhankelijk van steekproefgrootte n. Alternatief: sigma individuele metingen = sigma gemiddelden * sqrt(n). Excel-controle via STDEV op alle individuele metingen wordt in de oefeningen ook getoond ter vergelijking (bv. STDEV(B5:F24)=0,20212 vs Rbar/d2=0,20636 in oefening 2). (source/course/Les 4/2026 Lean - Six Sigma v13 - Capabiliteit - SPC.pptx p. 64, S06)
- **short-term vs long-term sigma / Zst vs Zlt**: If you are using a short-term standard deviation, the sigma (Z) score you calculate is a short-term sigma score Zst... If, however, you have a long-term standard deviation, you can calculate the long-term sigma score Zlt... Short-term variation performance, as quantified by the short-term sigma score Zst, represents the best variation performance that you can expect out of your currently configured process. It is an idealistic measure of capability. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 158, S08)
- **sigma estimate for capability (Cp/Cpk vs Pp/Ppk)**: Cp is a measure of short-term process or characteristic capability. Use only the short-term standard deviation to calculate its value. Using a long-term standard deviation in its calculation gives you incorrect results. ... The same capability indices that you calculate for short-term variation, Cp and Cpk, can also be calculated for long-term variation. To differentiate them from their short-term counterparts, these long-term capability indices are called Pp and Ppk. The only difference in their formulas is that you use sigma_LT in place of sigma_ST. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 163, S08)
- **Cpk rule of thumb**: Generally, a CPK greater than 1.33 indicates that a process or characteristic is capable in the short-term. Values less than this tell you that the variation is either too wide compared to the specification or that the location of the variation is offset from the center of the specification. Or it may be a combination of both width and location. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 164, S08)
- **control limits are not specification limits**: Never confuse control chart limits with specification limits! Specification limits -- like the USL and LSL introduced in Chapter 6 -- represent the voice of the customer. Control charts, however, represent only the voice of the process... control charts only determine whether the process is stable and predictable. They do not tell you whether the process is capable of meeting customer requirements. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 244, S08)
- **Cp / Cpk definitions**: Glossary: 'Cp: A capability measure defined as the ratio of the specification width to short-term process performance width.' 'Cpk: An adjusted short-term capability index that reduces the capability score in proportion to the offset of the process center from the specification target.' (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 338, S09)

### Control charts / SPC

- **n vs n-1 in standaardafwijking**: Standaardafwijking: s = sqrt( Σ_{i=1}^{n} (X_i - X̄)² / (n-1) ) — de slide toont expliciet de noemer (n-1) voor de steekproefstandaardafwijking. (source/course/Les 1/20260521_van volsem.pdf p. 131, S01)
- **Variable sampling plan: k factor and sigma known/unknown**: 'k is the counterpart of c for a single attributive plan'; the workbook and slides compute s as the sample standard deviation (sigma unknown, estimated from the sample: s = sqrt( 1/(n-1) * SUM(Xi-Xbar)^2 )), and set k ~= t(1-alpha,p0,n). (source/course/Les 2/20260529_ottoy_Acceptance Sampling.pdf p. 27, S04)
- **Bias-variance trade-off en SPC-analogie**: Bias-variance trade-off is de vaststelling dat wanneer je de bias-term probeert te verkleinen met een andere keuze van hyperparameters, je vaak zal zien dat de variance daardoor groter wordt, en vice versa. ... De bias-variance afweging is de statistische tegenhanger van het klassieke onderscheid tussen nauwkeurigheid (accuracy, ofwel bias) en precisie (precision, ofwel variance) in SPC (statistical process control). Een model met hoge bias en lage variance: consistent, maar systematisch verkeerd. Een model met lage bias en hoge variance: gemiddeld correct, maar onbetrouwbaar van meting tot meting. (source/course/Les 2/20261005_naert_web_lecture 2 notes.pdf p. 12, S11)
- **Controlelimieten breedte (3 sigma)**: UCL = gemiddelde + 3*sigma(xbar) => 3-sigma limiet, omgekeerd evenredig met steekproefgrootte n. Controlelimieten (UCL/LCL) zijn gebaseerd op procesprestatie (+/- 3 geschatte standaardafwijkingen van het gemiddelde) en zijn niet gelijk aan specificatielimieten (USL/LSL), die gebaseerd zijn op feedback van de klant. (source/course/Les 4/2026 Lean - Six Sigma v13 - Capabiliteit - SPC.pdf p. 56, S06)
- **Western Electric / Shewhart sensitizing rules**: Table 5.1 Some Sensitizing Rules for Shewhart Control Charts: (1) One or more points outside of the control limits. (2) Two of three consecutive points outside the two-sigma warning limits but still inside the control limits. (3) Four of five consecutive points beyond the one-sigma limits. (4) A run of eight consecutive points on one side of the center line. [1-4 = Western Electric Rules]. (5) Six points in a row steadily increasing or decreasing. (6) Fifteen points in a row in zone C (both above and below the center line). (7) Fourteen points in a row alternating up and down. (8) Eight points in a row on both sides of the center line with none in zone C. (9) An unusual or nonrandom pattern in the data. (10) One or more points near a warning or control limit. Zone A: between 2 sigma from centerline and control limit (3 sigma). Zone B: between 1 sigma and 2 sigma from centerline. Zone C: within 1 sigma of centerline. (source/course/Les 4/2026 Lean - Six Sigma v13 - Capabiliteit - SPC.pdf p. 68, S06)
- **Range- vs standaardafwijkingskaart (subgroepgrootte-drempel)**: For samples larger than 10, s-charts are recommended over R-charts, because s is much more efficient for estimating the process standard deviation sigma. For small samples and for manual maintenance of control charts, R is preferred over s. Slide-titel: 'Xbar/R- en Xbar/s-kaart (R small sample < 10)'. (source/course/Les 4/2026 Lean - Six Sigma v13 - Capabiliteit - SPC.pptx p. 73, S06)
- **n vs n-1 (variance/standard deviation)**: Notice that the mean (x̄) is included [in the variance formula]. This is where you lose the independence of one of your collected measurements. ... using the mean (x̄) reduces the number of independent measurements available to compute the variance. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 112, S07)
- **control-limit width**: There is an upper control limit (UCL): It's typically three process standard deviations above the average. There is a lower control limit (LCL): It's typically three process standard deviations below the average. ... Plus or minus three standard deviations from the mean includes 99.7 percent of all the data in a normally distributed population. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 241, S08)
- **control chart run rules (special-cause zone tests)**: Any one point beyond either control limit. Two out of any three consecutive points in Zone A, and all three on the same side of the process average. Four out of any five consecutive points in Zone B or A, and all five on the same side of the process average. Fifteen points in a row in Zone C, on either side of the process average. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 246, S08)
- **control chart pattern tests (Table 10-1)**: Stable and predictable: chart points do not form a particular pattern and lie within upper/lower control limits -> only common-cause variation. Beyond control limits: one or more points lie beyond UCL/LCL -> investigate special cause. Run: chart points on one side of centerline -> process has undergone a permanent change, may require recomputing control limits. Trend: continued rise or fall in a series of points (seven or more consecutive points in the same direction) -> gradual, cumulative special cause. Cycle: same pattern changes over equal periods of time -> cyclical, repetitive special cause. Hugging: points close to centerline or to a control limit line -> possible error in data subgrouping or selection. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 246, S08)
- **rational subgroup size guidance**: Rational subgroups are usually small in size, typically consisting of three to five measurements. ... After you have collected a minimum of 25 subgroups of data (with 2 to 5 measurements in each subgroup), you can calculate the statistics and control limits. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 248, S08)
- **continuous control chart pairing / choice by subgroup size**: Individuals and moving range (I-MR): subgroup size (n) = 1. Averages and ranges (Xbar-R): subgroup size 2-10. Averages and standard deviations (Xbar-S): subgroup size > 10. Use the Xbar-S chart when the size of your subgroups is ten measurements or greater, and the Xbar-R when they are less than ten. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 249, S08)
- **attribute control chart selection (p vs u)**: If you're charting defectives attribute data (pass/fail, go/no-go, acceptable/unacceptable), you use a p chart. ... If you're counting and keeping track of the number of defects on an item, you're using defect attribute data, and you use a u chart to perform statistical process control. p charts for defectives data are based on a binomial distribution. u charts for defects data are based on the Poisson distribution. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 254, S08)
- **rounding of the data ('afgeronde data' vs 'originele data')**: The Rheostat Knob Data workbook deliberately provides the same 27 subgroups of 5 measurements both 'The original data recorded in inches to 1/1000 inch' (sheet 'originele data') and 'Rounded data to 1/100 inch' (sheet 'afgeronde data'), specifically to illustrate the effect of coarse measurement resolution (inadequate discrimination) on the appearance of an X-bar/R control chart (course text, slide 30: 'Illustration : Rheostat knob data'). This is a teaching illustration of measurement unit granularity, not a general data-rounding convention for calculations elsewhere in the course. (source/course/Les 5/20260619_ottoy_Black Belt in Six Sigma - Measurement System Analysis.pdf p. 30, S10)

### Hypothesis tests and confidence intervals

- **Funneling / aantal Xs per DMAIC-fase (heuristiek)**: Define: >100 Xs; Measure: ≤20 Xs; Analyze: ≤6 Xs; Improve: ≤4 Xs; Control: ≤3 Xs. "DMAIC triggers the X from trivial many to significantly few". (source/course/Les 1/20260521_van volsem.pdf p. 73, S01)
- **Typische databronnen in de industrie (MTBF)**: Procesbeheersing, zoals temperatuurverliezen bij staalproductie; Betrouwbaarheid, zoals de Mean Time Between Failures (MTBF): de gemiddelde tijd tussen twee opeenvolgende pannes; Financiële resultaten: omzet, marge, kosten, … (source/course/Les 1/20261005_naert_web_lecture 1 notes.pdf p. 1, S11)
- **significance test = (1-confidence) of the equivalent CI**: 'When testing with a (1-alpha)x100%-CI, then the significance of the test is alpha' (Confidence Intervals.pdf p13); 'significantie test + betrouwbaarheid interval = 100%' (Testing of Hypotheses - Further Reading (Dutch) p17). (source/course/Les 2/20260529_ottoy_Confidence Intervals.pdf p. 13, S03)
- **normal approximation to binomial conditions**: Z-test voor pi requires n*pi0 > 5 ('slechts benaderd normaal, voorwaarde n*pi0 > 5'; if n*pi0<5 work with the exact distribution of P under H0) (Test Recipes - Further Reading (Dutch) p9-10). chi² 'goodness of fit' test requires e_k > 5 ('slechts benaderd chi², voorwaarde e_k > 5') (Test Recipes p16). chi² 'contingency tables' test requires e_kl > 5, with Yates continuity correction recommended for 2x2 tables (Test Recipes p19-20). The approximate CI for the fraction defectives similarly requires 'n sufficiently large means that at least 5 defectives need to turn up in the sample' (Confidence Intervals.pdf p10). (source/course/Les 2/20260529_ottoy_Test Recipes - Further Reading (Dutch).pdf p. 9, S03)
- **F-ratio orientation convention**: 'voor eenvoud van de testprocedure wordt de keuze van F best gemaakt rekening houdend met de beschikbare kritische waarden in de tabellen (enkel voor probabiliteiten < 0.5)' - i.e. F = s1²/s2² or F = s2²/s1² is chosen so the resulting F-value corresponds to an available (tail probability < 0.5) table entry (Test Recipes - Further Reading (Dutch) p14). Kritische waarden algemeen: F_alpha(n,m) = 1/F_(1-alpha)(m,n) (Test Recipes p12). (source/course/Les 2/20260529_ottoy_Test Recipes - Further Reading (Dutch).pdf p. 14, S03)
- **one- vs two-sided default**: No single default is stated; the course presents both a one-sided ('eenzijdige') and a two-sided ('dubbelzijdige') recipe as parallel, equally valid procedures, chosen according to the hypothesis: 'eenzijdige test geschikt om verschil in één richting te ontdekken; verschillen in beide richtingen ontdekken: dubbelzijdige test' and 'een eenzijdige test presteert wel beter in de gekozen richting: groter onderscheidingsvermogen of kracht' (Testing of Hypotheses - Further Reading (Dutch), p12/p14). Test recipes document formulates every specific test with both a (DZ) two-sided and (EZ) one-sided HA option. (source/course/Les 2/20260529_ottoy_Testing of Hypotheses - Further Reading (Dutch).pdf p. 12, S03)
- **generic 7-step test recipe (universal, applies to any single-sample test)**: Step 1: describe the problem as H0 and HA. Step 2: choose significance alpha. Step 3: determine an appropriate test statistic and its sampling distribution. Step 4: find the best sample size, taking into account error risks and practical constraints. Step 5: determine the reference distribution and the critical value (by measuring the significance on the reference distribution). Step 6: draw the sample from the population and determine the value of the test statistic. Step 7: compare the test statistic to the critical value and draw the conclusion. Test recipes differ from each other only in the choice of test statistic and its sampling distribution. (source/course/Les 2/20260529_ottoy_Testing of Hypotheses.pdf p. 9, S03)
- **p-value vs critical value approach**: Both approaches are presented as fully equivalent: 'Consequently, a decision can be made by directly comparing the p-value with the significance alpha, no need for a determining the critical value anymore' (Testing of Hypotheses.pdf p10); 'beter: rechtstreekse vergelijking p-waarde met significantie' (Testing of Hypotheses - Further Reading (Dutch) p11). (source/course/Les 2/20260529_ottoy_Testing of Hypotheses.pdf p. 10, S03)
- **pitfall: setting alpha after seeing the test statistic**: 'Set alpha after knowing the test statistic ... destroys the entire meaning of the test... The value of alpha should be said prior to starting the procedure and should be agreed upon between the stakeholders' (Testing of Hypotheses.pdf p11); 'alpha hangt af van economische context en wordt a priori bepaald' (Testing of Hypotheses - Further Reading (Dutch) p6). (source/course/Les 2/20260529_ottoy_Testing of Hypotheses.pdf p. 11, S03)
- **Lineaire regressie: OLS**: Lineaire regressie minimaliseert de MSE als loss-functie en heeft een analytische oplossing: de Ordinary Least Squares (OLS)-schatter. De coëfficiënten zijn interpreteerbaar en er bestaan geijkte statistische tests voor significantie. (source/course/Les 2/20261005_naert_web_lecture 2 notes.pdf p. 16, S11)
- **Slide: Bayesiaanse statistiek en frequentistische uitspraken**: In de frequentistische “klassieke” statistiek zijn hypothesen en parameters geen stochastische grootheden: “de kans dat de nulhypothese waar is” is geen zinnige uitspraak; “de kans dat de parameter β tussen 1 en 2 ligt” is geen zinnige uitspraak. Bayesiaanse statistiek: Parameters zijn zelf stochastische grootheden. (source/course/Les 2/20261005_naert_web_lecture 2 slides.pdf p. 53, S11)
- **Significance level (example-specific, no single stated course-wide default)**: Example 4.12 (paper tensile strength ANOVA) explicitly uses alpha=0.01 ('We will use alpha = 0.01'); separately, the 2^k effect confidence-interval rule references 'not significant at the alpha = 0.05 level'. The course does not state one single default alpha across this slice. (source/course/Les 3/20260605_de vuyst_BB_DOE.pdf p. 9, S05)
- **Significant figures / rounding when reporting regression coefficients**: Denk steeds na over het aantal beduidende cijfers dat je rapporteert. Excel trekt zich daar niets van aan, maar een ingenieur wel. (Always think about the number of significant digits you report. Excel does not care about this, but an engineer does.) (source/course/Les 3/20260605_de vuyst_BB_Regression.pdf p. 53, S05)
- **R^2 vs adjusted R^2 for model comparison**: R^2 always increases as more variables are added to the model (even if not significant); adjusted R^2 = 1-(1-R^2)(n-1)/(n-k-2) will only increase when the added variable is sufficiently significant, and is useful to compare different models: hoe hoger, hoe beter (the higher, the better) (source/course/Les 3/20260605_de vuyst_BB_Regression.pdf p. 56, S05)
- **alpha risk definition**: The risk of incorrectly concluding that the population average is within your calculated confidence interval when, in reality, it is not, is called alpha (a) risk. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 191, S08)
- **default confidence levels presented**: The book ties confidence directly to standard-deviation multiples: 68% corresponds to Z=1 (or t/chi2/F look-ups for one standard deviation), 95% to Z=2, and 99.7% to Z=3. Tables 8-1, 8-2, and 8-3 are built around these three levels (F table given only for 95%). No single universal alpha/confidence default is stated beyond these three illustrative levels. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 192, S08)
- **n vs n-1 (variance/std-dev confidence interval)**: Confidence interval for a standard deviation uses (n-1) sigma^2 in the numerator together with chi-square lower/upper look-up values (Table 8-2), i.e. based on n-1 degrees of freedom. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 195, S08)
- **DOE effect-to-coefficient convention**: The beta value for all... significant factors is found by dividing the corresponding effect value in half... It's because the effect value is calculated over a span of +1 to -1 for the variable. That's an effective distance of two, not one. Therefore, to get back to the right equation coefficient, you have to divide the calculated effect value by two. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 233, S08)
- **Gebruikte Excel-functienotatie (NL) voor statistiek**: Volledige lijst gedefinieerd in ___1.2 statistische functionaliteit in excel.pdf: o.a. NORM.VERD (=NORMDIST: kansdichtheid of cumulatieve verdelingsfunctie voor normaal model), NORM.INV (=NORMINV: inverse cumulatieve normale verdeling), NORMALISEREN (=z-waarde: (x-gemiddelde)/spreiding), Z.TOETS (eenzijdige Z-test voor mu, H0: mu=mu0 vs HA: mu>mu0), T.TOETS, T.INV, T.VERD, CHI.TOETS, CHI.KWADRAAT, F.TOETS, F.VERDELING, F.INVERSE, STDEV vs STDEVP, VAR vs VARP. (source/course/Les 4/___1.2 statistische functionaliteit in excel.pdf p. 1, S06)

### DOE / ANOVA / regression

- **Causale aannames uit observationele data**: Een interventie op een variabele X heeft alleen invloed op een andere variabele Y als er een causaal pad van X naar Y is. Uit observationele data alleen kunnen we het effect van een interventie in het algemeen niet bepalen. We hebben altijd bijkomende causale aannames nodig. (source/course/Les 1/20260522_naert_big data.pdf p. 39, S02)
- **Randomisatie als gouden standaard, met grenzen**: Randomisatie is de gouden standaard om causale effecten aan te tonen. ... maar het is niet altijd praktisch: ethisch (geen willekeurige toewijzing van roken), praktisch (geen willekeurig klimaat), financieel (geen willekeurige productieregimes voor weken). (source/course/Les 1/20260522_naert_big data.pdf p. 51, S02)
- **Regressielijn**: Dit is een functie die enkel afhangt van x, we noemen dit ook de regressielijn — het is één van de meest fundamentele concept uit de machine learning. (source/course/Les 1/20261005_naert_web_lecture 1 notes.pdf p. 12, S11)
- **Machine learning als schatting van voorwaardelijke verwachtingswaarden**: Veel machine learning-methoden — van eenvoudige lineaire regressie tot complexe neurale netwerken — zijn in essentie allemaal schattingen van de voorwaardelijke verwachtingswaarden (en soms van de volledige voorwaardelijke verdeling P(Y ∣ X)). Dit is het statistische fundament van alle supervised learning. (source/course/Les 1/20261005_naert_web_lecture 1 notes.pdf p. 13, S11)
- **Illustratie ijsverkoop en reddingsacties**: Een kustgemeente houdt bij hoeveel ijscrèmes ze per dag verkoopt (X) en hoeveel reddingsacties de kustwacht uitvoert (Y). Er is een duidelijke positieve correlatie ... Wat zou er gebeuren als de gemeente de ijsverkoop zou verbieden? Het antwoord is: helemaal niets — tenminste wat betreft het aantal verdrinkingen. Zowel de ijsverkoop als het aantal verdrinkingen worden aangedreven door een derde factor: het warme weer dat mensen naar het strand trekt. IJsverkoop is een geassocieerd met reddingsacties, maar geen oorzaak. (source/course/Les 1/20261005_naert_web_lecture 1 notes.pdf p. 17, S11)
- **Causale inferentie vereist aannames**: Uit observationele data alleen kunnen we het effect van een interventie in het algemeen niet bepalen. We hebben altijd bijkomende causale aannames nodig — die typisch afkomstig zijn uit domeinkennis of theoretische modellen. (source/course/Les 1/20261005_naert_web_lecture 1 notes.pdf p. 20, S11)
- **PINO-principe**: PINO: Prediction Is Not Optimization. Correlatie is geen causatie. ... het is niet omdat je er (correct) in slaagt om te observaties te voorspellen dat je de juiste acties zal voorstellen om een gewenst effect te bekomen. In de literatuur staat dit ook bekend als De “Cum hoc ergo propter hoc”-fallacy. (source/course/Les 1/20261005_naert_web_lecture 1 notes.pdf p. 21, S11)
- **Randomized Controlled Trial en A/B testen**: De gouden standaard voor het meten van causale effecten is de Randomized Controlled Trial (RCT): Deelnemers of eenheden worden willekeurig toegewezen aan een interventie- of controlegroep; Willekeurige toewijzing elimineert alle systematische verschillen: zelfs die variabelen waar we helemaal nog niet aan gedacht hebben; In een RCT geldt: P(Y ∣ do(X = x)) = P(Y ∣ X = x). In de industrie en e-commerce staat dit bekend als A/B testen: je verdeelt gebruikers, batches of periodes willekeurig over twee condities en meet het effect. (source/course/Les 1/20261005_naert_web_lecture 1 notes.pdf p. 24, S11)
- **Voorbeeld A/B test e-commerce**: Een webshop wil weten of een nieuwe productpagina de conversie verhoogt. Ze tonen de nieuwe pagina willekeurig aan 50% van de bezoekers en de oude aan de overige 50%. Na een week vergelijken ze de conversiepercentages. Het gemeten verschil is een causale schatting van het effect van de nieuwe pagina — niet enkel een associatie. (source/course/Les 1/20261005_naert_web_lecture 1 notes.pdf p. 24, S11)
- **Achterdeurpad en randomisatie**: Het pad T ← C → Y is een achterdeurpad: C is een gemeenschappelijke oorzaak van zowel T als Y. De geobserveerde associatie P(Y ∣ T) mengt daardoor het causale effect van T met de invloed van de confounders. Met randomisatie (RCT) ... Er bestaat nu geen pad meer van C naar T: alle achterdeurpaden zijn afgesneden. (source/course/Les 1/20261005_naert_web_lecture 1 notes.pdf p. 26, S11)
- **Do-calculus**: Do-calculus is een techniek bedacht door Judea Pearl die toelaat om interventionele effecten, zoals P(Y ∣ do(X)) uit te drukken als functie van normale, geobserveerde associaties. Niet elk interventioneel effect kan op deze manier geïdentificeerd worden maar als het mogelijk is, kan do-calculus het. (source/course/Les 1/20261005_naert_web_lecture 1 notes.pdf p. 27, S11)
- **Regression dilution / attenuation bias**: Dit effect staat bekend als regression dilution of attenuation bias: meetfouten in de onafhankelijke variabele leiden systematisch tot een onderschatting van de werkelijke relatie. ... Zelfs een ogenschijnlijk eenvoudige regressie-analyse kan leiden tot systematisch verkeerde conclusies indien men geen correct causaal model hanteert. (source/course/Les 1/20261005_naert_web_lecture 1 notes.pdf p. 29, S11)
- **Lineaire regressie: betekenis van "lineair"**: Lineair betekent dat het model lineair is in de parameters βi, niet noodzakelijk in de data. (source/course/Les 2/20260529_naert.pdf p. 34, S02)
- **Regressie versus classificatie**: Als y continu is, spreekt men van regressie. Het model leert typisch één waarde voorspellen voor een gegeven input: het model leert E[Y ∣ X = x]. Als y discreet is, spreekt men van classificatie. Het model leert typisch een kansverdeling voorspellen voor een gegeven input: het model leert P(Y ∣ X). (source/course/Les 2/20261005_naert_web_lecture 2 notes.pdf p. 3, S11)
- **Parameters versus hyperparameters**: Parameters (θ): de gewichten die het trainingsproces zelf optimaliseert. De analist hoeft ze niet handmatig in te stellen. Hyperparameters: keuzes die de structuur of complexiteit van het model bepalen — ze worden niet door de loss-minimalisatie bepaald, maar moeten vooraf ingesteld worden. Voorbeelden: Het aantal termen (graad) in een polynomiale regressie; Het aantal lagen en neuronen in een neuraal netwerk; De diepte van een beslissingsboom; De regularisatiesterkte λ bij ridge-regressie. - **Design resolution definitions**: Resolution III designs: no main effects aliased with one another, main effects aliased with two-factor interactions. Resolution IV designs: no main effect aliased with any other main effect or two-factor interaction; two-factor interactions aliased with each other. Resolution V designs: no main effects or two-factor interactions aliased with each other; two-factor interactions aliased with three-factor interactions. (source/course/Les 3/20260605_de vuyst_BB_DOE.pdf p. 85, S05)

- **DOE coding convention**: Using -1s as a code for the 'low' variable settings and +1s as a code for the 'high' settings, start with the left-most variable column, and fill in the column cells with alternating -1s and +1s. With the left-most column filled in, move on to the next column to the right and repeat the process -- but this time with alternating pairs of -1s and +1s. Fill in the next column to the right with alternating quadruplets of -1s and +1s, and so on. This table of patterned +1s and -1s is called coded design matrix. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 223, S08)
- **DOE randomize and block**: Always randomize the order of your experiment runs. This reduces the risk of extraneous variables skewing the results of your analysis. ... (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 224, S08)
- **Poka-Yoke corrective-action levels**: The most effective form of process control is sometimes called a Type-1 corrective action. This is a control applied to the process that eliminates the error condition from ever occurring. This is the primary intent of the Poka-Yoke method. The second most effective control is called a Type-2 corrective action. This is a control that detects when an error occurs and stops the process or shuts down the equipment so that the defect cannot move forward. This is the detection application of the Poka-Yoke method. (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 258, S08)

'short-term variation: The amount of variation observed in a characteristic that has not had the opportunity to experience all the sources of variation from the inputs acting on it.' (source/course/Les 4/Six Sigma For Dummies.pdf p. 340, S09)
